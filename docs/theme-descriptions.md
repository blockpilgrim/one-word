# Theme Word Descriptions

## Strong Examples

**Patience**
For when you keep trying to rush what can't be rushed. Patience is the discipline of trusting the process when you can't see the progress—staying with the slow work, resisting the urge to force a resolution, letting things unfold at their own pace. Choose this word when you're tired of fighting time and want to learn to work with it.

**Clarity**
For when everything feels muddy and you can't find the signal in the noise. Clarity is about cutting through—knowing what you actually think, what you actually want, what actually matters. It's the willingness to stop hedging and see things as they are. Choose this word when you're ready to trade comfortable confusion for sharper vision.

**Flow**
For when you're tired of forcing things. Flow is about finding the current instead of fighting it—whether that means losing yourself in focused work, softening your grip on how things *should* go, or learning to move through transitions without bracing against them. It's the opposite of stuck. Choose this word when you want to stop pushing so hard and start noticing where the energy already wants to move.

---

## Structure

1. **Opening:** "For when..." names a recognizable frustration or state the reader is in
2. **Middle:** Defines the word through 2-3 concrete expressions, often using dashes or parallel structure to show breadth without exhaustive listing
3. **Optional:** A short, punchy sentence that crystallizes the idea
4. **Closing:** "Choose this word when..." reframes the opening or offers a forward-looking version of it

## Tone

- Second person throughout
- Conversational but not casual—contractions are fine, slang is not
- Poetic phrasing used sparingly, balanced by plain language
- Validates the reader's current state rather than promising transformation
- No imperatives until the final sentence

## Length

3-4 sentences, roughly 50-80 words

## Avoid

- Abstract or dictionary-style definitions
- Exhaustive lists of every possible interpretation
- Promises or outcomes ("You'll feel more at peace")
- Mystical or woo-adjacent framing
- Overly prescriptive language
